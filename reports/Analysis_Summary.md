# **Analysis Summary**

## **1. Inventory Analysis**

The inventory portfolio consists of 2,010 SKUs, representing 4.84 million inventory units with a total inventory value of $46.0 million. Supplier lead times range from 1 to 290 days (average 29.82 days), while supplier on-time delivery performance ranges from 70% to 100% (average 90.23%). The portfolio also exhibits an average demand variability (COV) of 0.8798, indicating that inventory planning should account for uncertainty in future demand. Demand trend analysis further shows that 1,336 SKUs (66.47%) are classified as growing-demand products, representing approximately 70.93 million units of projected annual demand and $35.56 million in inventory value, while the remaining 674 SKUs (33.53%) are classified as declining-demand products, representing approximately 7.62 million units of projected annual demand and $10.44 million in inventory value.

The inventory replenishment analysis indicates that 1,564 SKUs (77.81%) are currently below their reorder points, 108 SKUs (5.37%) are near their reorder points, and only 338 SKUs (16.82%) remain above their reorder points. These groups represent approximately $40.88 million, $1.08 million, and $4.05 million in inventory value, respectively. The results suggest that a substantial portion of the inventory portfolio has reached its replenishment threshold and should be reviewed by inventory planners.

The inventory value analysis examines the distribution of inventory investment across the portfolio. The SKU with the highest on-hand inventory value (56722.42673) accounts for $4,876,665, representing 10.60% of the total inventory value. The second- and third-ranked SKUs (64992.5687 and 77066.7326) contribute 3.03% and 2.46%, respectively. Among all 2,010 SKUs, only 11 items individually account for more than 1% of the total inventory value, indicating that inventory investment is concentrated in a relatively small number of products.

The ABC inventory classification further confirms this concentration. Class A contains only 208 SKUs (10.35% of the portfolio) yet represents 80.07% of the total inventory value. In contrast, Class C accounts for more than 70% of all SKUs but contributes only 5.00% of the total inventory value. This suggests that inventory control efforts should primarily focus on the relatively small group of high-value Class A items.

Inventory coverage varies substantially across the portfolio. Thirty-two SKUs have more than one year of projected inventory coverage, including eight SKUs with more than 100 months of coverage. The highest observed inventory coverage exceeds 1,500 months, indicating potential excess inventory situations for a small number of items. These items collectively represent approximately 50,140 potential excess inventory units, equivalent to approximately $434,430 in potential excess inventory value. Conversely, 1,357 SKUs have less than one month of projected inventory coverage. The estimated inventory value required to restore these SKUs to one month of projected coverage totals approximately $36.86 million. The largest individual shortfall exposure represents approximately $2.40 million (6.50% of the total estimated replenishment value), while the top three SKUs collectively account for 17.64% of the total exposure. These findings indicate that the financial impact of inventory shortages is concentrated in a relatively small number of products, providing a practical basis for prioritizing replenishment decisions.

Safety stock analysis shows that the highest safety stock requirement is 375,478 units, representing 11.22% of the product's projected annual demand. Across the top 20 inventory items, safety stock requirements range from approximately 16,000 to 375,000 units. No single planning variable consistently explains the highest safety stock requirements, indicating that buffer inventory is determined by the combined effects of projected demand, demand variability, and replenishment characteristics.

The supplier portfolio consists of 12 suppliers generating approximately $74.34 billion in total revenue and $6.78 billion in cash from operations. Average supplier on-time delivery performance is 84.67%, with an average credit rating of 2.92 and an average data security score of 8.0. Half of the suppliers (6 suppliers, 50.00%) are single-source suppliers, while 8 suppliers (66.67%) operate without IP protection. Additionally, 2 suppliers (16.67%) have reported labor unrest, and 2 suppliers (16.67%) have experienced environmental incidents. These portfolio-level characteristics establish the baseline for subsequent supplier assessment.

Supplier on-time delivery performance ranges from 63% to 94%, indicating noticeable variation across the supplier portfolio. Boavidro records the lowest on-time delivery performance at 63%, followed by BestOGlass (78%) and PharmyLeaf (79%). These suppliers may require closer monitoring because lower delivery reliability can increase operational uncertainty and disrupt production schedules.

Regarding supplier dependency and information security, six suppliers are identified as single-source suppliers, representing 50.00% of the supplier portfolio. These suppliers operate across the United States, China, and the Philippines, indicating that single-source dependency is geographically distributed rather than concentrated in a single region. However, all suppliers with IP protection are classified as single-source suppliers, although not every single-source supplier has IP protection (4 out of 6). In addition, data security scores among single-source suppliers range from 7 to 10, indicating varying levels of information security within this supplier group.

Additionally, of all twelve suppliers, three suppliers exhibit labor unrest or environmental incidents. RealGlass is the only supplier associated with both labor unrest and environmental incidents, while Plaxian and Shale each exhibit one of these indicators.

The binary risk indicator combines single-source dependency, lack of IP protection, labor unrest, and environmental incidents into a simple descriptive screening measure. RealGlass and Plaxian exhibit the highest number of binary risk indicators (3), followed by BestOGlass and Shale (2). Rather than representing a validated supplier risk model, the binary indicator count is intended to highlight suppliers exhibiting multiple qualitative risk characteristics that may warrant additional review.

Operating cash flow margins range from 0.45% to 100%, although 11 of the 12 suppliers fall between 0.45% and 23.76%. MedicMetric reports the highest operating cash flow margin at 100%, followed by PharmyLeaf (23.76%) and Optikiet (20.35%). In contrast, BestOGlass reports the lowest operating cash flow margin at 0.45%.

The quantitative performance profile complements the binary risk indicators by normalizing credit rating, delivery performance, and data security onto a common 0–100 performance scale, where higher scores indicate stronger supplier performance. An equally weighted composite performance score is calculated solely for descriptive comparison across suppliers. Under this framework, basicPharm records the highest overall performance score (86.00), while RealGlass records the lowest (60.67), primarily because of its weak credit rating despite strong delivery and data security performance. These results demonstrate that supplier performance is multidimensional, with strengths in one area not necessarily compensating for weaknesses in another.

## **3. Production Planning and Manufacturing Capacity**

The production portfolio consists of three product lines: Ampoules, Syringes, and Vials. Projected demand increases continuously across all planning periods for every product, indicating sustained growth in production requirements. Among the three products, Ampoules consistently exhibit the highest projected demand in every planning period, followed by Syringes and Vials. Vials require the longest overall production cycle (63 hours), while Syringes have the shortest cycle time (57 hours). Although Vials require the longest production cycle per unit, Ampoules are expected to require the greatest production capacity based on the Q2 2021 demand projection, totaling approximately 3,900 production hours, followed by Syringes (2,565 hours) and Vials (2,205 hours). Across all three product lines, packing is the most time-consuming production process, requiring 24 hours for each product. In terms of quality performance, contamination represents the highest reported reject rate for all three products, ranging from 5.2% to 6.0%, making contamination the most frequently observed defect category across all three product lines.

Ampoules maintain the highest projected demand throughout the planning horizon, increasing from 15 units in Q3 2020 to 65 units in Q2 2021. During the same period, Syringes increase from 10 to 45 units, while Vials increase from 5 to 35 units. Although Vials have the lowest projected demand, they exhibit the strongest relative growth (600%), compared with Syringes (350%) and Ampoules (333%). Despite the substantial initial increase between Q3 and Q4, quarter-over-quarter growth rates gradually moderate across all three products, indicating that demand is projected to continue increasing at a slower pace over time.

Quality performance varies only slightly across the three product lines. Vials exhibit the highest summed reported defect rate at 15.0% (average 3.75% across the four reported defect categories), followed by Syringes (14.0%) and Ampoules (13.0%). Across all three products, contamination is consistently the largest reported defect category, ranging from 5.2% to 6.0%, indicating that contamination represents the primary quality challenge regardless of product type.

The manufacturing network consists of 14 production units with a total of 2,015 reported non-operating days, including 1,610 weekend and holiday days, 140 planned shutdown days, and 265 unplanned shutdown days. Scheduled downtime is consistent across all production units, with each unit reporting 115 weekend and holiday days and 10 planned shutdown days. Consequently, differences in total non-operating days are entirely attributable to unplanned shutdowns, which range from 15 to 25 days. Forming #2 and Packing #3 experience the highest unplanned downtime (25 days), resulting in the highest total non-operating days (150 days) among all production units. Overall, nine production units exceed the manufacturing-wide average of 143.93 non-operating days, indicating that downtime is unevenly distributed across the manufacturing network.
